A frozen 3D U-Net is adapted to nine brain-MRI tasks from six public cohorts with 32 labelled volumes each, comparing LoRA, bottleneck adapters, bias-only tuning, linear probing, full fine-tuning and the continual-learning remedies EWC, LwF and replay under one subject-grouped, whole-volume protocol over five seeds (676 runs). Which strategy to use is decided by a property of the new task that is known before training: whether it inherits a large task-specific head. The four segmentation tasks do (glioma, stroke, hippocampus, white-matter hyperintensities), and there training that decoder on the frozen encoder is at least as accurate as LoRA, which trails by at most 0.015 Dice while storing 88x less per task. The five global tasks do not (brain age, sex and dementia on IXI and OASIS-1), and there only the strategies that move the encoder representation (LoRA, bottleneck adapters) learn at all: linear probing and bias-only tuning return a constant output even though their frozen features rank subjects well above chance. The shape of the rule reproduces on a second backbone, a Swin UNETR with a self-supervised transformer encoder. Freezing is also the only route to zero forgetting: a second segmentation task erases the first under full fine-tuning in either order, and EWC, LwF and replay do not prevent it. Pretraining proves a small, task-dependent effect, so a random-initialisation control should accompany any adopted checkpoint.
