Adding a new analysis task to a deployed 3D brain-MRI model usually means a few dozen annotated cases, no access to earlier tasks' data, and a shared backbone. We compare eleven adaptation strategies, from LoRA, bottleneck adapters, bias-only tuning and linear probing to full fine-tuning, EWC, LwF and replay, on nine tasks from six public cohorts (four segmentation tasks; brain age, sex and dementia on IXI and OASIS-1) at 32 labelled volumes each, under one subject-grouped whole-volume protocol (five seeds, 676 runs, seed-level inference). What the new task inherits decides the strategy. Segmentation inherits a large decoder: training it on a frozen encoder is as accurate as LoRA on every task and better on one, and a frozen decoder with only its 66-parameter output projection trainable collapses. The global tasks inherit a ~1K-parameter head, and their frozen features are more usable than gradient training suggests: a gradient-trained probe returns a constant, while the same probe fitted in closed form on standardised features matches LoRA on sex classification and comes within 0.7 yr of it on IXI brain age; moving the encoder pays only on the harder OASIS cohort. Full fine-tuning at its own learning rate is the most accurate single-task option, but a second task erases the first; EWC reduces the loss by a third without removing it, and only freezing shared weights gives zero forgetting. The pretrained checkpoint loads 59 of 82 segmentation tensors and is indistinguishable from random initialisation under LoRA on four tasks.
